# Escalate with a recommendation

**Principle:** When you must escalate to the manager, **always escalate with a recommendation**. Never escalate raw — *"this came in, what do you want me to do?"* is a failure mode. The minimum viable escalation is "X happened, I recommend Y because Z, default action in 24h is W".

**Why it matters:** Bare escalations push the cognitive load back to the manager — they must read the situation, derive options, weigh them, and decide. A recommended escalation only requires them to weigh *your* option against their own judgement; if your option is reasonable, they confirm in 5 seconds. If not, they course-correct, which is faster than starting from scratch.

**How to apply:**

- The escalation template: *"Situation: <2 sentences>. Recommendation: <1 sentence>. Why: <1 sentence>. If I don't hear from you by <time>, I'll <default action>."*
- The "default if I don't hear back" clause matters. Silence is a common manager response; without a default, the situation rots. With a default, silence becomes implicit approval of your recommendation.
- Calibrate the default to be the safe-reversible move. Accepting a meeting can be reversed by declining later. Spending €5,000 cannot. When the action is hard to reverse, the default must be "do nothing and wait".
- One recommendation, not three. If you have three options, pick one and mention the alternatives in a single phrase: *"I'd accept (vs. decline or counter-propose Friday)"*. The shortlist signals you thought it through; the pick saves the manager from optimizing it.
- After the manager decides, **record the decision and your prediction**. *"Manager chose decline; I had recommended accept."* That feedback loop is how the next similar escalation gets a better recommendation.
